Verus

AI-powered ground penetrating radar analysis for bridge inspection

A 1D CNN that reads ground penetrating radar signals to find hidden defects in bridge decks

Verus is an AI-powered data analysis platform for subsurface inspection of bridges, buildings, and other infrastructure. Inspectors collect ground penetrating radar (GPR) data, and Verus runs every signal through a convolutional neural network to flag likely delamination and estimate rebar depth, turning about 80 hours of manual review into minutes. I co-founded Verus and built the model and the full-stack platform around it. It grew out of the Discovery Project and my earlier work on FluxSpace.

How It Works

  • Ingest: converts raw GPR formats (DZT, SEG-Y, DT1, RD3/RD7) and GPS data into a common signal format
  • Model: a PyTorch 1D CNN classifies each signal for delamination; the production model went through 17 versions. A second model estimates rebar depth, with a physics-based fallback
  • Training data: about 658,000 signals from five bridges in the SDNET2021 dataset, plus synthetic data from a numpy Ricker-wavelet generator and gprMax physics simulations, plus real Georgia Tech bridge scans
  • Output: per-signal probabilities are assembled into C-scan condition maps and rebar depth maps for each inspected structure
  • Platform: a FastAPI inference server on Render with a job queue, a React and TypeScript frontend with Mapbox maps, and Supabase for auth, projects, and storage
