About

I'm Aiden Erard, a Computer Engineering student at Georgia Tech. I chose Computer Engineering because I didn't want to choose between hardware and software. I wanted to understand both.

Most of my time outside class goes into personal projects. I've built SOAR, an autonomous drone concept focused on public safety; GridGuard, a power monitoring system; and Tempr, an AI-powered music app that uses Gemini AI and the Spotify API to generate playlists from your mood, photos, or calendar. The project I invested the most in is Verus, an AI-powered data analysis platform for subsurface infrastructure inspection that I co-founded. The core idea is using technologies like GPR, ultrasound, and thermal imaging to collect data from structures like bridges, then running that data through AI and ML models to surface insights that would otherwise require a lot of manual interpretation. I built a 1D CNN trained on more than 663,000 annotated GPR signals, along with the full-stack platform around it, which cut bridge deck analysis from about 80 hours of manual review to 5 minutes. We raised $155,000 and ranked in the top 10% of the YC Winter and Spring 2026 batches and in the PearX Summer 2026 batch.

On the academic side, I started with CS 1301 and CS 1331, which built my foundation in Python and object-oriented programming in Java. The robotics labs in 1301 were extremely helpful in learning how to use Python in robotics settings. I programmed iRobot Create 3 robots to solve mazes and play pong. This semester I'm taking CS 1332 on data structures and ECE 2035, where I'm writing C and RISC-V assembly.

Outside of class, I'm a software engineer intern at Theseus (YC S24), where I work on sensor fusion for GPS-denied drone navigation. I also do undergraduate research in Georgia Tech's LiDAR Lab on bipedal robot navigation over challenging terrain, and I volunteer as a prototyping instructor at the Invention Studio.

I'm still figuring out where I want to focus, whether that's robotics, embedded systems, autonomous vehicles, or something I haven't gotten to yet. I want to keep building things that are useful and keep pushing myself technically.

Outside of that, I follow developments in AI and robotics and pick up new tools when something looks interesting. Traveling is something I genuinely love, whether it's somewhere new or somewhere I've been before. This past summer I studied abroad with Georgia Tech-Lorraine in Metz, France, and it's one of those things I know I will always do.

Career Goals

I'm early enough that my goals are still taking shape, but after thinking about what excites me most, here's where I think I'm headed.

Long-Term Vision

Long term, I want to work on autonomous systems, whether that's in robotics, aerospace, or vehicles, and build a company of my own. Co-founding Verus was my first step in that direction, and it showed me what it takes to turn a hard technical problem into a company. I'm drawn to the idea of finding a hard technical problem and building a company around solving it rather than waiting for the right moment after graduation.
